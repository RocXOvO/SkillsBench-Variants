\subsection{Generalization under Task Variations}
\label{sec:variant-transfer}

We evaluate source-task skills under changes to input representation (P1), task-instance structure (P2), and coupled task requirements (P3). The final record covers 222 task variants and 886 condition-level results across no skill and the three methods, including completed recovery evaluations. For each method, Table~\ref{tab:variant-transfer} summarizes the recorded reward difference from a no-skill execution of the same variant.

\paragraph{SkillEvolver.} Across 222 variants, mean recorded reward increases from 41.87 to 53.81, a mean within-variant gain of 11.94 points, with 47 improvements and 20 regressions. Gains remain positive under all three types of change: 11.85 points on P1, 14.56 on P2, and 9.72 on P3. The strongest gain occurs when the task instance changes, consistent with learned procedures adapting beyond the original inputs.

\paragraph{SPARK.} Across 221 variants, mean recorded reward increases from 41.61 to 42.25, a mean within-variant gain of 0.65 points, with 32 improvements and 37 regressions. Gains are 2.00 points on P1 and 4.53 on P3, while P2 produces a 5.23-point decline.

\paragraph{CoEvoSkills.} Across 221 variants, mean recorded reward decreases from 41.61 to 36.60, a mean within-variant change of $-5.01$ points, with 26 improvements and 41 regressions. Changes are $-3.59$ points on P1, $-9.51$ on P2, and $-2.42$ on P3. The positive gains on standard tasks do not extend to the changed tasks in this summary.

SkillEvolver maintains positive gains across all three variation types, ranging from 9.72 to 14.56 points. The execution traces below show how its skills preserve diagnostic steps and task constraints when inputs and requirements change.

\subsection{Tracing the Source of Transfer}

Execution traces connect the aggregate result to concrete decisions encoded in the skills. In exoplanet period detection, SkillEvolver separates candidate discovery from refinement and checks whether a dominant peak reflects stellar modulation or a transit. After the input columns are reordered in P1, the executor rejects the approximately 1.395-day modulation and recovers the accepted 5.35907-day period. When P3 requires two signals, the same frozen skill supports a narrower-duration search and refinement to 2.40052 and 5.35958 days. CoEvoSkills' end-to-end period routine encounters duration-grid errors in P1 and returns the stellar-modulation period; in P3 it selects approximately 1.40 days and its 0.70-day harmonic as separate signals. SkillEvolver succeeds on P1 and P3, while CoEvoSkills fails both. On the replacement-data P2 instance, both skills receive zero, giving the case a specific transfer pattern across the three changes.

Scheduling locates the difference at a constraint that determines correctness. SkillEvolver's artifact explicitly preserves baseline-relative right shifts and precedence-aware local minimality. The executor carries these rules across renamed inputs, a replacement scheduling instance, and an added machine-handoff requirement, passing all three variants; SPARK also passes all three. CoEvoSkills passes P3, but its P1 output fails the local-minimality check: an operation starts at 29 with an anchor of 24 and can move earlier. The other structural and policy checks pass. This trace-based attribution connects the outcome to preservation of a repair invariant. Together, the cases illustrate a transferable skill as a set of diagnostic and constraint-preserving decisions that the executor adapts to the new task.
